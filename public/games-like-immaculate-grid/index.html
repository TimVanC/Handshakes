7 Games Like Immaculate Grid

Updated August 2026 · All free, all daily

Immaculate Grid nailed the formula: one puzzle a day, nine guesses, and just enough obscurity that finishing a grid clean feels earned. But one grid takes five minutes, and then you're staring at tomorrow. If your morning routine has room for more sports trivia, these seven daily games scratch the same itch — some are grids, some are mystery-player guessers, and all of them reward the kind of fan who remembers where a guy played before he got good.

Full disclosure: the first one is ours. It's also the reason this page exists.

1. Journeyman

NBANFLMLBMystery player

A mystery journeyman's career, revealed one jersey at a time. Each day you get a player who bounced around the league, and his career unfolds as a spread of trading-card jerseys — team colors, jersey number, years of each stint. The fewer jerseys you need before naming him, the better your score. Where Immaculate Grid tests whether you can produce a player who fits two criteria, Journeyman flips it: here's the career, name the man.

There are three separate dailies — NBA, NFL, and MLB — so it stacks neatly with a grid habit.

2. Poeltl

NBAMystery player

The original NBA Wordle, now run officially with the NBPA and named after Jakob Poeltl himself. Eight guesses to find the mystery player; every guess tells you whether you've matched his team, conference, division, position, height, age, and jersey number, with a silhouette for the truly stuck. It leans current-roster, so it plays faster and more forgiving than a grid.

3. Weddle

NFLMystery player

The NFL's answer to Poeltl, named after safety Eric Weddle. Guess the mystery player and get feedback on division, position, height, age, and jersey number. Positional knowledge matters a lot here — narrowing down which of thirty-two teams' depth charts you're fishing in is most of the game.

4. MLB Pickle

MLBMystery player

Formerly WARdle, rebranded to the much better name MLB Pickle. Nine guesses at a mystery big-leaguer, with feedback on team, position, handedness, and birth year. Baseball's enormous player pool makes this one genuinely hard on deep-cut days — in a good way.

5. HoopGrids

NBAGrid

The closest thing on this list to Immaculate Grid itself, but all basketball, every day. Same 3×3 format — nine guesses, match players to the intersection of two teams or a team and an award. If the NBA edition of Immaculate Grid is your favorite, this doubles your supply.

6. Crossover Grid

Multi-sportGrid

A daily 3×3 grid that rotates across sports — and occasionally out of sports entirely, with movie and pop-culture editions. The rarity scoring (uncommon correct answers score better) pushes you past the obvious superstar answer, which is where grid games get fun.

7. Puckdoku

NHLGrid

The hockey grid. Nine cells, two criteria each, and a player pool full of fourth-liners who played for six franchises — hockey might be the sport best suited to the format. If you know your deadline-deal rentals, this is your game.

FAQ

What is Immaculate Grid, exactly?

A daily 3×3 trivia grid from Sports Reference: each row and column names a team or criterion, and you get nine guesses to fill every cell with a player who fits both. It started with baseball and now has editions for the NBA, NFL, NHL, and soccer.

Are these games free?

Yes — every game on this list is free to play in the browser, one puzzle per day, no download.

Which one should I try if I'm tired of the grid format?

The mystery-player games flip the mechanic. Journeyman shows you a career and asks for the name; Poeltl, Weddle, and MLB Pickle give you attribute feedback on each wrong guess. If you can look at "Sixers–Suns–Rockets–Clippers" and feel a name forming, start with Journeyman.
